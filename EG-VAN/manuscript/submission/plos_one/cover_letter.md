Please consider our Research Article, “EG-VAN+ as a Reliability-Extension Prototype: Seven-Class HAM10000 Evaluation and PH² External Follow-up.” The study independently reconstructs a dual-branch skin-lesion classifier and evaluates reliability around its frozen checkpoint through technical image-quality proxies, saved-probability calibration and uncertainty, validation-derived exploratory review, bounded qualitative Grad-CAM, and external follow-up. The intended image-quality accept/reject gate was not validated. The article does not claim author-code identity, a matched replication of the original paper's accuracy, or clinical readiness.

The recovered Stage 15 epoch-16 checkpoint was selected by frozen HAM10000 validation criteria. On 1,014 HAM test images, full-cohort accuracy was 0.823471, macro F1 0.699582 and macro one-versus-rest AUC 0.958206; melanoma recall was 56/107 (52.34%). A validation-derived entropy review rule retained 805/1,014 cases with 90.93% accuracy among retained cases and sent 106/179 errors, including 23/51 melanoma false negatives, to review. These conditional results do not change the full-cohort accuracy. In the distinct PH² external follow-up, melanoma recall was 11/40 (27.5%). PH² had prior project use. The missing original epoch-16 weights prevent a byte-identity claim despite exact replay of available observables.

We believe the transparent methods, complete classwise errors and qualified generalization result fit PLOS ONE's emphasis on sound, reproducible research, including informative negative findings.
