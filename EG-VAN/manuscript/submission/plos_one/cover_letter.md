Dear PLOS ONE Academic Editor,

Please consider our Research Article, “Independent EG-VAN reconstruction for seven-class skin lesion recognition: HAM10000 test and PH² follow-up.” The study independently reconstructs a dual-branch skin-lesion classifier and reports its validation-selected seven-class performance, numerical execution correction, and separate external-domain follow-up. The article relates to the published EG-VAN architecture but addresses a different, seven-class task and does not claim author-code identity or a matched performance comparison.

The recovered Stage 15 epoch-16 checkpoint was selected by frozen HAM10000 validation criteria before final evaluation. On 1,014 HAM test images, accuracy was 0.8235, macro F1 was 0.6996 and macro one-versus-rest AUC was 0.9582. Melanoma recall was 56/107 (52.34%); 41 of 51 missed melanomas were called nevus. In a distinct 120-case PH² external follow-up, melanoma recall was 11/40 (27.5%) and melanoma-probability AUC was 0.5428. PH² had prior use in the broader project and is not presented as untouched external validation. The missing original epoch-16 weight file precludes proof of byte identity with the recovered checkpoint, despite exact replay of available observables. No clinical-readiness claim is made.

We believe the transparent methods, complete classwise errors and qualified generalization result fit PLOS ONE's emphasis on sound, reproducible research, including informative negative findings.
