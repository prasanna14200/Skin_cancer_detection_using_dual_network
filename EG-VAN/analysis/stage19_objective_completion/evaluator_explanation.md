# EG-VAN / EG-VAN+ explanation for a project evaluator

EG-VAN classifies dermoscopic images using two feature-extraction branches: EfficientNetV2S and a modified ResNet50 with attention. Their features are combined before a seven-class prediction. We reconstructed this architecture and trained and selected one final checkpoint using the fixed HAM10000 validation split.

Our larger EG-VAN+ goal was reliability as well as classification. An image may be blurred, badly illuminated, or hard to see; a quality screen could flag it before a prediction. A model can also make a confident-looking but wrong prediction, so uncertainty and calibration analysis could help identify cases needing review. Testing on data from another source matters because a model that works on its development dataset may transfer poorly.

The final frozen model achieved **82.35% accuracy** and **0.6996 macro F1** on 1,014 HAM test images, with **0.9582 macro one-versus-rest AUC**. It correctly recognized **56 of 107 melanomas**; 41 of the 51 missed melanomas were called benign nevus (NV). The original EG-VAN paper's approximately **98.20% accuracy** is the paper's reported result, not our reproduced result. Its reported class task and our seven-class, lesion-separated protocol differ, so the percentages are not a like-for-like comparison.

We also evaluated the unchanged final model on a mapped PH² follow-up cohort: 80 common nevi and 40 melanomas, with 80 atypical nevi excluded by the fixed protocol. It correctly identified **11 of 40 melanomas** and reached **66.67% overall accuracy** in the included cohort. PH² had already been used elsewhere in the project, so we call this an **external follow-up**, not untouched external validation. The lower melanoma result shows limited transfer in this evaluated setting; it does not prove a particular cause or clinical outcome.

The repository includes image-quality measurements, confidence/calibration analyses and Grad-CAM visualizations, but these were largely completed on **earlier baseline models**. Quality measurements covered processed HAM images and were associated descriptively with baseline errors; they were not turned into a validated pre-model accept/flag gate. Final EG-VAN predictions include seven probabilities, making CPU-only uncertainty and calibration analysis possible without retraining, but that final-model analysis and an evaluated review rule have not yet been completed. Existing Grad-CAM examples explain an earlier EfficientNetV2S checkpoint, not the final EG-VAN candidate.

The final model needed one numerical correction: a non-local attention calculation could overflow in FP16, so its q@k multiplication ran in FP32 while the rest retained AMP. This enabled stable execution; no accuracy improvement is claimed from the correction. The selected epoch-16 checkpoint was recovered by an exact observable replay, although the missing original weight file prevents a byte-identity claim.

**Conclusion for the viva:** the EG-VAN reconstruction and frozen HAM/PH² evaluations are complete, and the external melanoma gap is an important negative result. The full EG-VAN+ reliability objective is **partially complete** because a final-model quality gate and evaluated uncertainty-based review pathway are absent. We would report the verified results as they are. If the thesis requires those reliability features, the next work is a prospectively defined, limited analysis using saved validation/final probabilities and separately justified image-quality labels—not another attempt to tune the model toward the paper's number.
