# Hypotheses for a future, separately authorized controlled study

**Not selected from PH2 performance.** The existing Stage 23 model remains frozen. These are hypotheses drawn from HAM training/validation diagnostics; they are not prescriptions to modify the current model or to inspect test/PH2 outcomes during selection. One candidate only could be preregistered under [the conditional protocol](CONTROLLED_DEVELOPMENT_PROTOCOL.md), after a clean internal boundary and confirmation plan exist.

| Rank | One-factor hypothesis | HAM-only supporting observation | Cost and bias risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A **single predeclared mild training-only Gaussian blur augmentation policy**, relative to the Stage 23 training policy, could reduce clean-image melanoma errors while preserving macro-F1 and MEL specificity. | On the *same* 986 HAM validation images, fixed synthetic blur reduced MEL recall sharply and increased MEL→NV errors. This is perturbation sensitivity, **not** proof augmentation will help. | Two full T4 runs for fresh control/treatment; moderate-to-high GPU cost. High risk of overfitting if blur strength/probability is repeatedly chosen from the already viewed Phase B results. Fix one mild policy on training rationale **before** the new selection outcomes; no sweep. |
| 2 | A **single predeclared mild training-only contrast or exposure augmentation policy** could reduce sensitivity to technical brightness/tonal shifts without sacrificing clean seven-class performance. | HAM Phase B fixed underexposure and contrast reduction reduced MEL recall, while Phase A brightness/contrast proxies were confounded by lesion properties. The signal is weaker and mechanism unresolved. | Two full T4 runs; moderate-to-high GPU cost. High risk of turning synthetic stress-test outcomes into a parameter search. Choose one operator and strength prospectively; do not combine operators under an apparent one-change claim. |

Neither hypothesis is presently approved for implementation. Melanoma/NV confusion and unreliable entropy motivate the **primary MEL recall and specificity guardrail** in the future protocol, but do not identify a causal training fix. Stage 14/24/25 already show that validation trade-offs can favor aggregate metrics while harming melanoma behavior; another ad hoc loss, LR or sampler sweep on the same exposed validation data is not justified. No accuracy gain is promised. A new patient-linked independent cohort is required to compare any future candidate with the original frozen Stage 23 checkpoint.
