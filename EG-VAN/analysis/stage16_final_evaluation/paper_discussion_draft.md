# Discussion draft

The frozen Stage 15 candidate provided good internal multiclass discrimination on HAM10000: seven-class macro OVR AUC was 0.9582 and macro F1 was 0.6996. AUC reflects ranking across thresholds, whereas the reported classwise recall and F1 use the frozen seven-class argmax rule. The latter exposed moderate melanoma sensitivity (56/107, 52.34%) despite 82.35% overall accuracy. The large nevus class (676/1,014 test images) contributed strongly to aggregate accuracy and makes classwise results essential to interpretation.

Melanoma–nevus confusion was the dominant clinically relevant error pattern: 41 melanoma cases were predicted NV, accounting for 80.39% of melanoma false negatives. BKL was the next most frequent melanoma destination (five cases). Conversely, 18 nevi were predicted MEL. These transitions suggest a difficult boundary among pigmented lesion classes in this fixed evaluation, but the saved predictions alone do not identify whether acquisition, lesion morphology, preprocessing, or label uncertainty caused individual errors. The high DF precision also requires restraint because the class contained only 11 test examples.

Under the established PH² follow-up mapping, melanoma recall fell to 11/40 (27.5%), while 18/40 melanomas were called NV and 11/40 were assigned other HAM classes (all BKL). The melanoma-probability AUC was 0.5428. This is evidence of weaker performance in the evaluated external domain, not proof of a particular causal domain-shift mechanism. PH² differs from HAM in image source and true-label space; only common nevi and melanomas were mapped, and atypical nevi were excluded. Its prior use elsewhere in the project also means it is an external follow-up cohort rather than untouched external validation. Differences between PH² and HAM overall accuracy should not be interpreted as matched-population effects.

The selective FP32 execution of the nonlocal3 q@k affinity addressed a demonstrated FP16 overflow while the surrounding model remained under CUDA AMP. That numerical correction allowed bounded stability checks and the guarded Stage 15 run; it was applied as an execution policy, not a change to the scientific loss or sampler factors. It does not establish improved classification performance, and numerical stability alone cannot ensure melanoma generalization.

The selected epoch-16 checkpoint was recovered from the archived epoch-13 state after the original epoch-16 weights failed to persist. Replayed epochs 14–16 matched saved observable history and validation artifacts exactly, and all recovery manifest hashes were verified. Since the original weight file is unavailable, its byte identity to the recovered weights cannot be proven. Final test and follow-up results apply to the frozen recovered checkpoint specifically.

No threshold, checkpoint, preprocessing, or architecture change followed test or PH² evaluation. Subsequent writing should preserve these frozen evaluation scopes. Any future model development would require a separately designed study and evaluation data that are not used for selection in this closed experiment.
