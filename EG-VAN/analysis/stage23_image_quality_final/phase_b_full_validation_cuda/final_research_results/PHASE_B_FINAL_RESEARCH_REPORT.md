# Stage 23 Phase B: full-validation synthetic image-degradation study

## Research question and scope

This study asks how a **frozen, validation-selected Stage 23 EG-VAN classifier** behaves when its raw validation images undergo six fixed synthetic degradations before the established preprocessing pipeline. The working robustness hypothesis was that degraded images would produce more classification errors, and the uncertainty hypothesis was that predictive entropy would rise as image conditions became less favorable. The experiment tests synthetic perturbation sensitivity; it does not validate real-world technical adequacy or a clinical quality gate.

The Stage 23 epoch-14 checkpoint was frozen at SHA256 `42b018305694392ea874c1e9a4b28457adef780f7be9e740a16506404c9fc5ab`. The cohort comprises all **986** images and **743** distinct lesions in the frozen Stage 23 validation split, including **107** melanomas and **663** nevi. The seven classes are `akiec, bcc, bkl, df, mel, nv, vasc`. No HAM test outcomes or PH² images/outcomes were used. No model training, threshold tuning, or checkpoint selection occurred.

## Frozen intervention and execution

The predeclared conditions were unchanged baseline; Gaussian blur of radius 1 or 2 source pixels; raw RGB brightness multiplied by 0.70 or 1.30; contrast reduced to 0.70 around the global RGB mean; and a pre-preprocessing JPEG quality-40 round trip with subsampling 0. Each intervention preceded the frozen hair-removal, Gray World and Retinex preprocessing. The resulting images then used the original RGB resize-to-384, tensor and normalization path. Baseline read the original processed JPEG directly with PIL, matching the Stage 23 validation loader, with its historical SHA256 and exact RGB pixel identity checked.

The complete run used a Tesla T4, PyTorch `2.11.0+cu130`, CUDA FP16 autocast, physical batches of 16 in the frozen validation order, and the checkpoint's selective FP32 `resnet.nonlocal3` q@k numerical policy. It saved atomic batch commits, then a 6,902-record combined prediction CSV. The baseline gate required exact argmax agreement and seven-class probability delta ≤0.005 against the saved Stage 23 reference. It passed for **986/986 cases with maximum delta 0.0**; all six degraded runs followed that clearance. The runner reported 434 complete committed batches across seven conditions.

Independent download audit confirmed the checkpoint, split, reference predictions, source and protocol hashes; every manifest-listed output hash; and all 434 committed batch metadata/record hashes. The read-only final-table integrity check passed for **6,902/6,902** records, with 986 unique IDs under each condition. The earlier analyzer error was caused by machine-precision CSV reserialization of derived scalar fields, not probability or prediction disagreement. The current check compares all seven probabilities exactly and bounds only that redundant scalar round-trip noise. Full details and file hashes are in `PHASE_B_REPRODUCIBILITY_AUDIT.md` and `PHASE_B_ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md`.

## Classification response to synthetic degradation

Baseline validation accuracy was **82.96%**, macro-F1 **0.6713**, and melanoma recall **70/107 (65.42%)**. Every fixed degradation reduced accuracy and macro-F1 relative to that same-image baseline. Paired lesion-cluster 95% percentile intervals for both metrics were below zero under each condition. Radius-1 blur reduced accuracy by 10.55 percentage points (pp; interval −13.43 to −7.81) and MEL recall by 50.47 pp (interval −60.58 to −40.70). Radius-2 blur reduced accuracy by 13.79 pp (interval −17.03 to −10.44) and MEL recall by 63.55 pp (interval −74.77 to −51.56), leaving **2/107** melanomas correctly identified. Underexposure and contrast reduction produced smaller aggregate accuracy losses (3.35 and 4.26 pp) but marked MEL-recall losses (34.58 and 40.19 pp). JPEG quality 40 reduced accuracy by 6.80 pp and MEL recall by 29.91 pp.

Overexposure reduced overall accuracy by 14.50 pp and macro-F1 by 0.2318, while MEL recall was 67/107 rather than 70/107. Its paired MEL-recall interval (−9.80 to +3.74 pp) includes zero. This does not establish a reproducible MEL-specific decline for that condition, even though aggregate classification worsened. The asymmetry across classes matters: under radius-2 blur, NV recall rose from **625/663 (94.27%)** at baseline to **658/663 (99.25%)**, while MEL recall collapsed. Aggregate accuracy of 69.17% under that condition therefore obscures a severe melanoma error pattern. The saved classwise results for all seven classes are in the original `cuda_statistical_results.json`.

The six degradation conditions flipped between **133 and 273** of the 986 predictions (13.49–27.69%). The paired correct-to-incorrect and incorrect-to-correct counts, all metrics, exact values and full intervals are in `PHASE_B_RESULTS_SUMMARY.md` and `condition_comparison.csv`. The intervals were generated by 1,000 lesion-cluster bootstrap resamples; they describe sampling uncertainty for this validation cohort and these fixed perturbations. They are not uncertainty bounds for external datasets or deployment. Multiple comparisons were exploratory and not multiplicity adjusted.

## Melanoma failure analysis

At baseline, 37/107 melanomas were false negatives; 26 were predicted as NV. Radius-1 blur increased MEL false negatives to **91**, including **74 MEL→NV**. Radius-2 blur increased them to **105**, including **95 MEL→NV**. Of the 70 baseline-correct melanomas, 54 became wrong under radius-1 blur and 68 under radius-2 blur; neither blur condition recovered a baseline-wrong melanoma. Underexposure changed 37 baseline-correct MEL cases to wrong, and contrast reduction changed 43. JPEG quality 40 changed 33 to wrong and recovered one. Overexposure changed 10 to wrong and recovered seven. These transition counts are paired at the **image level**. The interval calculations resample **lesions**, preserving their repeated-image grouping; the two units must not be conflated.

These changes show pronounced sensitivity of melanoma classification to some fixed synthetic perturbations and a strong tendency toward NV predictions under blur. They do not reveal why a particular lesion was misclassified, whether the synthetic parameters match actual acquisition defects, or whether any image should be rejected clinically.

## Predictive uncertainty

Predictive entropy did **not** increase consistently with worsening classification. Mean entropy *fell* by 0.1414 nats with radius-1 blur, 0.0640 with underexposure, and 0.0841 with contrast reduction; their lesion-cluster intervals were below zero. It rose by 0.2037 with overexposure and 0.1085 with JPEG quality 40; both intervals were above zero. Radius-2 blur produced a small mean rise of 0.0201 nats with an interval crossing zero. Thus a simple entropy-increase rule would miss some substantial synthetic degradation effects, including the radius-1 blur collapse in MEL recall.

Within each condition, entropy still had descriptive value for ranking incorrect versus correct predictions: error-detection AUROC was **0.8349** at baseline and ranged from **0.7418 to 0.8440** under degradation. Baseline median entropy was 0.4084 nats for correct predictions and 0.9532 for incorrect predictions. This within-condition association is different from identifying degraded images. The error-detection AUROCs have no reported intervals and were calculated on the same validation set; they do not support a validated clinical quality detector or a new review threshold. Softmax entropy is model uncertainty, not a human technical-quality label.

## Relation to the 21-image Phase B pilot

The earlier pilot deliberately selected three lesions per class (21 images total) and ran on CPU. All 21 images are in this full cohort, and their **147 matched predicted labels** (21 × seven conditions) agree with the CUDA run. The pilot qualitatively anticipated accuracy losses for every degradation, especially blur-associated melanoma losses. Its class-balanced, very small sample produced much larger blur accuracy-drop estimates than the full validation cohort and only three melanoma observations. The pilot's entropy-change directions agree with the full cohort, but a full-cohort interval for radius-2 blur spans zero. Pilot overexposure left its three melanomas correctly classified; the full cohort had a small net MEL loss with an interval spanning zero. Neither result supports a strong condition-specific MEL claim for overexposure. These pilot/full comparisons are descriptive, not independent replication.

## Limitations and interpretation

The study uses a single frozen model and one previously used validation partition. Synthetic changes are controlled but do not model all real blur, exposure, compression, device, skin-tone, lesion-visibility or workflow effects. In particular, global brightness/contrast changes and Gaussian blur are image operations, not adjudicated adequacy labels. The baseline/degradation execution paths also differ as specified: baseline uses the historical processed JPEG, whereas degraded images pass through raw-image intervention and frozen preprocessing. The exact baseline probability replay supports model/runtime consistency, but the experiment does not establish image-quality ground truth.

The cohort has class imbalance and 107 melanoma images; some lesion IDs have repeated images, hence cluster resampling. Multiple condition and class comparisons are exploratory. No human reviewers graded quality, no prospective or external-domain quality evaluation was performed, and no clinically validated accept/reject cutoff follows from these results. The Stage 23 checkpoint was neither changed nor newly selected on these outcomes. No HAM test or PH² outcome was accessed in this final audit.

## Supported conclusion and next research step

The **robustness part** of the research hypothesis is supported on this frozen validation cohort: all six controlled degradations reduced accuracy and macro-F1, and several sharply reduced melanoma recall. The **uniform uncertainty-response part is not supported**: entropy sometimes declined while error rates rose. Entropy can rank errors within a condition, but it is not a demonstrated technical-quality measure.

The most justified next experiment is a **separately preregistered, blinded technical-quality annotation and validation study** on a new image cohort: use at least two independent reviewers with adjudication and lesion-level grouping, define focus/exposure/obstruction/visibility/adequacy labels before seeing model errors, then test whether the current quality proxies and entropy predict those labels and whether degradation sensitivity transfers. Do not use HAM test or PH² outcomes to choose a quality gate or optimize this frozen classifier. Until such evidence exists, present Phase B as synthetic robustness evidence and the image-quality gate objective as incomplete.
